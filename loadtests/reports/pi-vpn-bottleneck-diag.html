Finding: the workstation↔Pi link has spare capacity (iperf3 ≈907 Mbps, LAN sink ≈848 Mbps on eth1 1000). The ≈60–95 Mbps ceiling comes from OpenVPN/CPU on the Pi (4× Cortex-A72 @ 1.8 GHz), below the gigabit link capacity.
Purpose. Channel-ramp showed nearly flat aggregate Mbps and falling per-client throughput. This run isolates the causes: raw link capacity, VPN encryption, session capacity and fairness.

Specific limits

  • Link (shared throughput): aggregate VPN throughput reaches ≈ 60–95 Mbps (B: 94 with 2 streams; D: 63 with 50; channel-ramp: 60–75 with 25–250). Per-client throughput falls because clients share the same aggregate capacity.
  • Session capacity: stable at 250 without traffic; at 500, only ~40–75% connect (211/500 here; 384/500 with traffic in channel-ramp — variation reflects Pi load and state).
  • Throughput ≠ capacity: N=250 connect-only reaches 100% with almost no traffic; at N=50 under load, aggregate throughput remains comparable to 2–4 heavy streams.
  • Link versus CPU (confirmed): opened :9200 from LAN (iptables-nft + legacy) and iperf3 :5201. LAN ≈ 850–907 Mbps, VPN ≈ 96 Mbps → OpenVPN encryption/CPU on the Pi is the bottleneck, with spare eth1 capacity.

Terminology

  • Link / agg Mbps — total bits across all clients ÷ elapsed time: shared aggregate throughput.
  • Per-client throughput (p50) — each client’s share. It falls as N grows when aggregate throughput is flat.
  • Tunnel capacity — the number of Initialization Sequence Completed events, without requiring traffic.
